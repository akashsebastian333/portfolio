Cross-site message impersonation and recovery-channel stripping via the ask.pinterest.com proxy

Form-POST CSRF family — no CSRF token, no Origin check. probing…

While you are logged in to Pinterest in this browser, this page fires form writes at the ask.pinterest.com proxy with your session cookies. The proxy forwards v3 gateway writes without the X-CSRFToken layer Pinterest's own www resource layer enforces — so an ordinary cross-site form suffices. Beyond the account-takeover chain (separate PoCs), the same channel lets any website send Pinterest messages as you and strip your phone-based recovery/2FA channel. The channel is chosen automatically: when third-party cookies are attached (step 1 read succeeds), writes go to a hidden iframe and stay silent; when they are blocked (Safari/ITP, Firefox strict, Brave, Chrome third-party phaseout), the same writes fire as top-level navigations in one reused window and the final action lands you back on pinterest.com.

Root cause: ask.pinterest.com/api/proxy/* forwards v3 reads and writes with the user's SameSite=None session cookies and no CSRF layer at all, while www.pinterest.com/resource/* enforces a double-submit X-CSRFToken header (form posts there are redirected to /csrf_error/ — verified). Fix: enforce the same header or an Origin allowlist on the proxy, or restrict the proxy to GET.
